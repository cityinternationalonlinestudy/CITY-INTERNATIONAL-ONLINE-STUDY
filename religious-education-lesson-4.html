🕊️ Forgiveness and Peace

Learn about forgiveness, peaceful relationships, patience and healthy ways of solving disagreements.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the meaning of forgiveness.
  • Explain what peace means.
  • Identify peaceful ways of solving disagreements.
  • Explain why patience and listening are important.
  • Know when to seek help from a trusted adult.

💚 What Is Forgiveness?

Forgiveness means choosing to let go of anger or resentment after someone has caused harm or hurt. Many religious traditions teach forgiveness as an important part of healthy relationships.

Forgiveness does not mean that harmful behaviour was acceptable. It also does not mean that a person must remain in an unsafe situation.

Important:

If someone is being hurt, threatened or bullied, they should tell a trusted adult and seek help.

🕊️ What Is Peace?

Peace means living and working together without unnecessary violence, hostility or conflict. Peace includes respect, understanding and cooperation.

Ways to promote peace

👂
Listen

Give people an opportunity to explain their views.

💬
Communicate

Use calm and respectful language.

🤝
Cooperate

Look for solutions that are fair and constructive.

❤️
Respect

Treat other people with dignity.

💬 Solving Disagreements Peacefully

People may disagree because they have different ideas, feelings or interests. A disagreement does not have to become a fight.

Try these steps:

1. Stay calm

Take a moment before responding.

2. Listen

Allow the other person to explain their point of view.

3. Speak respectfully

Explain your feelings without insulting or threatening anyone.

4. Look for a solution

Try to find a fair and peaceful way forward.

5. Ask for help when necessary

Talk to a teacher, parent or another trusted adult when the problem cannot be safely solved.

⏳ Patience

Patience means staying calm when something takes time or when a situation is difficult.

Being patient can help us think clearly instead of reacting angrily. It can also help us listen carefully and make better decisions.

Example:

If two students disagree about whose turn it is, they can remain calm, listen to each other and ask a teacher for help if necessary.
