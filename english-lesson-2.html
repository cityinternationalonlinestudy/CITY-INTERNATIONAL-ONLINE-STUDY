Sentence Structure

Learn how words are organized to make clear, correct and meaningful sentences.

🎯 Lesson Objectives

  • Understand what a sentence is.
  • Identify the subject and predicate.
  • Understand the basic sentence pattern.
  • Recognize simple and compound sentences.
  • Identify different types of sentences.
  • Use correct capitalization and punctuation.
  • Understand basic subject-verb agreement.

📖 What Is a Sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought. A sentence normally begins with a capital letter and ends with punctuation such as a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark.

Example:
The boy is reading a book.

The words above form a complete thought, so they make a sentence.

👤 Subject and Predicate

Subject

The subject tells us who or what the sentence is about.

Example:
Sarah is singing.

Sarah is the subject because the sentence is about Sarah.

Predicate

The predicate tells us what the subject does or what happens to the subject.

Example:
Sarah is singing.

Is singing is the predicate.

🧩 Basic Sentence Pattern

A common English sentence pattern is:

Subject + Verb + Object
Example:
John + eats + an apple.
Another example:
The teacher + teaches + Mathematics.

The subject performs the action, the verb shows the action, and the object receives the action.

📝 Simple Sentences

A simple sentence contains one main idea and normally has one independent clause.

Examples:

The baby sleeps.
Mary plays football.
The students study English.

Each sentence expresses one complete idea.

🔗 Compound Sentences

A compound sentence joins two complete ideas. Words such as and, but, and so can be used to connect them.

Example:
I wanted to play, but it was raining.
Example:
Sarah studied hard, so she passed the test.
Example:
John opened the book and he started reading.

📚 Types of Sentences

1. Declarative Sentence

A declarative sentence makes a statement.

The school opens at eight o'clock.

2. Interrogative Sentence

An interrogative sentence asks a question.

Where do you live?

3. Imperative Sentence

An imperative sentence gives a command or instruction.

Open your book.

4. Exclamatory Sentence

An exclamatory sentence expresses strong feeling.

What a beautiful day!

🔠 Capitalization and Punctuation

Every sentence should normally begin with a capital letter. We also use punctuation marks to show how a sentence ends.

Full stop:
I like English.
Question mark:
Do you like English?
Exclamation mark:
That is wonderful!

✅ Subject-Verb Agreement

The subject and verb should agree in number.

Correct:
She plays football.
Correct:
They play football.

Remember that singular subjects often use a verb ending in -s in the present tense.

📌 Lesson Summary

  • A sentence expresses a complete thought.
  • The subject tells us who or what the sentence is about.
  • The predicate tells us what the subject does or what happens.
  • A common pattern is Subject + Verb + Object.
  • Simple sentences express one main idea.
  • Compound sentences join two complete ideas.
  • Sentences need correct capitalization and punctuation.
  • The subject and verb should agree.
