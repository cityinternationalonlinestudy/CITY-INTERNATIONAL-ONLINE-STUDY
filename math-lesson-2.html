Fractions

Welcome to Lesson 2. In this lesson you will learn what fractions are, how to identify their parts, compare fractions and perform simple fraction operations.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what a fraction is.
  • Identify the numerator and denominator.
  • Recognize proper and improper fractions.
  • Identify equivalent fractions.
  • Compare simple fractions.
  • Add fractions with the same denominator.

1. What is a Fraction?

A fraction represents a part of a whole.

For example, if a pizza is divided into 4 equal pieces and you take 1 piece, you have taken one-fourth of the pizza.

We write one-fourth as:

1 4

The number at the top tells us how many parts we have. The number at the bottom tells us how many equal parts make the whole.

2. Numerator and Denominator

3 5
Numerator: The top number is called the numerator.

In 3 5 the numerator is 3.

Denominator: The bottom number is called the denominator.

In 3 5 the denominator is 5.

3. Types of Fractions

Proper Fraction

The numerator is smaller than the denominator.


2 5

Improper Fraction

The numerator is equal to or greater than the denominator.


7 4

Mixed Number

A whole number combined with a proper fraction.


2 1 3

4. Equivalent Fractions

Equivalent fractions have different numbers but represent the same value.

For example:

1 2 is equivalent to 2 4 and 4 8
Example: To change 1 2 into an equivalent fraction with denominator 4, multiply both the numerator and denominator by 2.

1 × 2 = 2
2 × 2 = 4

Therefore:
1 2 = 2 4

5. Comparing Fractions

When fractions have the same denominator, compare their numerators.

Example: Which is greater?

3 7 or 5 7

Since 5 is greater than 3:

5/7 is greater than 3/7.

6. Adding Fractions with the Same Denominator

When fractions have the same denominator, add the numerators and keep the denominator.

Example:

2 7 + 3 7 = 5 7

We add:
2 + 3 = 5

The denominator remains 7.
