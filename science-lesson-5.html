Science Lesson 5

Matter and Materials

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what matter is.
  • Identify the three common states of matter.
  • Describe the properties of solids, liquids and gases.
  • Give examples of different states of matter.
  • Explain melting, freezing, evaporation and condensation.
  • Identify common materials and their properties.
  • Explain why different materials are used for different purposes.

1. What Is Matter?

Definition: Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space.

Everything around us is made of matter. The chair you sit on, the water you drink, the air you breathe and the food you eat are all examples of matter.

Examples of Matter

  • Water
  • Air
  • Wood
  • Stone
  • Metal
  • Plastic
  • Food

2. States of Matter

Matter commonly exists in three states: solid, liquid and gas.

🧱

Solid

A solid has a fixed shape and a fixed volume.

Examples:
  • Stone
  • Book
  • Chair
  • Ice
💧

Liquid

A liquid has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container.

Examples:
  • Water
  • Milk
  • Juice
  • Cooking oil
💨

Gas

A gas does not have a fixed shape or fixed volume.

Examples:
  • Air
  • Water vapour
  • Oxygen
  • Carbon dioxide

3. Comparing the Three States

State Shape Volume Example
Solid Fixed Fixed Stone
Liquid Not fixed Fixed Water
Gas Not fixed Not fixed Air

4. Changes of State

Matter can change from one state to another when it gains or loses heat.

Melting

Melting happens when a solid changes into a liquid because of heating.

Example: Ice → Water

Freezing

Freezing happens when a liquid changes into a solid because it loses heat.

Example: Water → Ice

Evaporation

Evaporation happens when a liquid changes into a gas, usually because of heating.

Example: Water → Water vapour

Condensation

Condensation happens when a gas changes into a liquid as it cools.

Example: Water vapour → Water

Simple Example

When ice is heated, it melts and becomes water. When water is heated strongly, some of it becomes water vapour. When water vapour cools, it can become liquid water again.

5. Materials

Material: A material is a substance from which an object is made.

Objects can be made from different materials depending on the properties needed.

Common Materials

  • Wood
  • Metal
  • Plastic
  • Glass
  • Rubber
  • Cloth
  • Paper

6. Properties of Materials

Materials have different properties. These properties help us decide how a material should be used.

Property Meaning Example
Hard Difficult to scratch or bend Metal
Soft Easy to press or change shape Cotton
Flexible Can bend easily Rubber
Waterproof Does not allow water to pass through easily Plastic
Transparent Allows light to pass through Clear glass
Strong Can withstand large forces Steel

7. Uses of Materials

We choose materials according to their properties.

Examples

  • Metal is used for cooking pots because it can withstand heat.
  • Glass is used for windows because transparent glass allows light to pass through.
  • Rubber is used for tyres because it is flexible and provides grip.
  • Wood is used for furniture because it is strong and can be shaped.
  • Plastic is used for many containers because it is lightweight and can resist water.
  • Cotton is used for clothes because it is soft and comfortable.

8. Simple Mixtures

A mixture is formed when two or more substances are combined without forming a new substance.

Examples

  • Sand and water
  • Salt and water
  • Rice and beans
  • Oil and water

Separating Mixtures

Some mixtures can be separated using simple methods.

  • Hand picking: picking different objects apart by hand.
  • Sieving: using a sieve to separate particles of different sizes.
  • Filtration: using a filter to separate a solid from a liquid.
  • Evaporation: removing water from a solution by heating.
