Tenses

Learn how verbs show when an action happens — in the past, present, or future.

🎯 Lesson Objectives

  • Understand what a tense is.
  • Identify past, present, and future time.
  • Understand the simple present tense.
  • Understand the simple past tense.
  • Understand the simple future tense.
  • Use tenses correctly in sentences.

📖 What Is a Tense?

A tense is a form of a verb that helps us understand when an action happens.

Tense tells us WHEN an action happens.

The three basic time periods are:

  • Past — an action happened before now.
  • Present — an action happens now or regularly.
  • Future — an action will happen later.

🟢 1. Simple Present Tense

We use the simple present tense for actions that happen regularly, habits, routines, and facts.

Subject + Verb
Examples:

I go to school every day.
She plays football.
They study English.
The sun rises in the east.

Important Rule

With he, she, or it, the verb often takes -s or -es.

He plays football.
She watches television.
John goes to school.

🔵 2. Simple Past Tense

We use the simple past tense for actions that happened and finished in the past.

Subject + Past Verb
Examples:

I walked to school yesterday.
She played football last week.
They visited their grandmother.
John watched a movie.

Regular Verbs

Many regular verbs form the past tense by adding -ed.

play → played
walk → walked
clean → cleaned
visit → visited

Irregular Verbs

Some verbs change differently in the past.

go → went
eat → ate
see → saw
come → came

🟣 3. Simple Future Tense

We use the simple future tense for actions that will happen later.

Subject + will + Verb
Examples:

I will study tomorrow.
She will visit her grandmother.
They will play football next week.
We will learn English.

📊 Comparing the Three Basic Tenses

Past

I played football yesterday.

Present

I play football every Saturday.

Future

I will play football tomorrow.

⏰ Time Words

Certain words can help us identify the tense being used.

Past:
yesterday, last week, last year, ago
Present:
every day, usually, always, often, sometimes
Future:
tomorrow, next week, next year, later

⚠️ Common Mistakes

❌ She play football.
✅ She plays football.
❌ Yesterday I go to school.
✅ Yesterday I went to school.
❌ Tomorrow I went to school.
✅ Tomorrow I will go to school.

📌 Lesson Summary

  • Tense shows when an action happens.
  • Past tense describes completed actions in the past.
  • Present tense describes current, regular, or factual actions.
  • Future tense describes actions that will happen later.
  • Some past verbs end in -ed.
  • Some verbs have irregular past forms.
  • We commonly use "will" for the simple future.

✏️ Practice Questions

1. Change this sentence to the past tense:

"I play football."

Answer: I played football.
2. Change this sentence to the future tense:

"She studies English."

Answer: She will study English.
3. Complete the sentence:

"John ___ to school every day."

Answer: goes
4. Complete the sentence:

"Yesterday, Mary ___ a book."

Answer: read
